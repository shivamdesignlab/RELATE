Where seven models point their users

Results from 1,596 dialogues and 69,194 assistant sentences, judged by a rubric-based LLM judge. Every rate is the share of sentences that meet a criterion.

  • Across models
  • Across turns
  • Across user styles
  • Judge sensitivity
  • Scope

  • Inward-facing language leads in six of seven models.

    Inward-facing rates ran from 56.2% to 71.9%, outward-scaffolding from 41.6% to 62.3%. Mistral-7B was the one exception.

  • It climbs with conversational depth.

    Pooled across models, inward-facing language rose from 40.5% of sentences at turn 1 to 72.9% at turn 6, and it rose in every model.

  • Hesitant users get less outward support.

    Outward-scaffolding was 12.8 points lower for hesitant, indirect users than for explicit ones, while inward-facing language stayed level.

Rates pooled over all six turns. Read each row from the cobalt point (outward-scaffolding) to the coral point (inward-facing); the right column gives the difference.

Across turns

The same dialogues, read one assistant turn at a time. Both directions grow as conversations deepen, but inward-facing language grows faster in six of seven models.

Across user styles

The same 76 situations, voiced three ways. P1 is hesitant and indirect, P2 discloses while minimizing, and P3 is explicit and seeks reassurance.

The styles vary jointly in disclosure, self-minimization, and reassurance-seeking, so the gap cannot be attributed to disclosure level alone.

Sensitivity to the judge

We re-judged a stratified 10% sample with GPT-4o: 161 dialogues, 23 per model, 6,827 sentences.

Both judges find higher inward-facing rates at turn 6 than at turn 1, and both order outward-scaffolding the same way across styles, lowest for P1 and highest for P3. They differ in calibration: GPT-4o marks far fewer sentences as outward-scaffolding, and the two disagree on the direction of its change across turns. Sentence-level agreement was 68.1% for inward-facing (κ = 0.33) and 71.0% for outward-scaffolding (κ = 0.42).

What these numbers do and don't say

  • They describe language, not outcomes. Inward-facing and outward-scaffolding characterize what models say. They do not establish effects on dependence, wellbeing, or relationships; that needs studies with real users.
  • Neither direction is good or bad on its own. Whether a given balance is appropriate depends on the person and the context.
  • User styles are interactional conditions, not people. They should not be used to characterize anyone who discloses hesitantly or seeks reassurance.
  • The rates are automated estimates. They come from LLM judges, six-turn dialogues, a shared prompting protocol, and one serving configuration per model.
