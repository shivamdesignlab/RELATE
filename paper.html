RELATE: An Evaluation Framework for Measuring Relational Orientation of Large Language Models

Abstract

Large language models are increasingly used for emotional support, raising concern that sustained use may draw users away from their real-world relationships. Yet existing evaluations focus primarily on the safety, empathy, or helpfulness of responses, leaving a relational question under-examined: where does the model orient the user for continued support?

We introduce relational orientation, a property operationalized through two non-exclusive dimensions: inward-facing language, which positions the AI as the user's ongoing source of support, and outward-scaffolding language, which encourages real-world human connection. Grounded in psychological and sociological literature, we formalize a taxonomy of relational orientation and present RELATE, a persona-conditioned framework for measuring both at the sentence level in multi-turn dialogues.

RELATE pairs 76 help-seeking situations, adapted from naturally occurring questions, with three simulated user styles, giving 228 evaluation stimuli. We evaluate seven LLMs in dialogues of six assistant turns, yielding 1,596 dialogues and 69,194 assistant sentences, assessed by a primary rubric-based LLM judge with a secondary judge on a subset. Under automated evaluation, the share of sentences labeled inward-facing is higher at the sixth assistant turn than at the first, while the share labeled outward-scaffolding is substantially lower for hesitant, indirect simulated users than for explicit, reassurance-seeking ones. RELATE provides a reproducible framework and a sentence-level signal for auditing and steering the relational orientation of supportive LLMs.

Contributions

  • A construct and a taxonomy

    Relational orientation describes how supportive language positions the assistant and other people as sources of continued support. Drawing on psychological and social theory, we define four inward-facing categories and an outward-scaffolding criterion.

  • A persona-conditioned framework

    RELATE operationalizes the taxonomy through simulated multi-turn dialogues and contextual, sentence-level evaluation, enabling comparisons across models, conversational turns, and user styles.

  • An empirical evaluation

    Across seven models, inward-facing rates are higher at the final turn than at the first, and outward-scaffolding rates are lower for hesitant, indirect users than for explicit, reassurance-seeking ones.

Ethics

RELATE measures properties of model language. Its labels do not establish that a response is helpful, harmful, safe, or clinically appropriate, and the three user styles are interactional conditions rather than demographic or clinical profiles.

No users were recruited and no new personal conversations were collected. Dialogues were generated in a controlled simulation from transformed CounselBench-Eval situations, and released artifacts contain none of its text, the rewritten stimuli, or simulated-user messages.
